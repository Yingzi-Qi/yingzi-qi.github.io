My story

A path shaped
by questions.

From mathematics and physics to global health, with an interest in how ideas are understood along the way.

A few chapters so far
China & the UK
Warwick / Oxford
01 / Foundations

Learning to look closely.

I studied mathematics and physics at Warwick. The combination gave me a foundation in mathematical modelling, numerical methods and working through a problem from its underlying principles.

For my independent dissertation, I used experimental X-ray reflectivity data and published literature to understand thin-film structure and behaviour. It brought together modelling, uncertainty and the task of explaining what the evidence could support.

02 / A new context

Taking that thinking into health.

My interest in public health began during my undergraduate degree. I went on to study Modelling for Global Health at Oxford, where I explored how quantitative methods can help us understand health problems.

Moving into a new field meant learning quickly and asking questions. In my research placement at the Big Data Institute, I worked on transmission patterns using anonymous NHS COVID-19 App data, taking responsibility for an analytical workstream within a research team.

03 / A visual thread

Making room for another way of seeing.

Before university, I studied art alongside mathematics and physics. That is another part of my background: an interest in how an idea is presented, as well as how it works.

My independent web projects bring those interests closer together. They let me explore how information can be organised and made interactive, from comparing remittance costs to exploring a fictional business’s assumptions.

04 / Looking ahead

Still curious about what comes next.

My experiences in China and the UK contribute to my interest in access and opportunity. I want to keep developing my quantitative skills, learn from experienced colleagues and contribute to work with a useful purpose.
