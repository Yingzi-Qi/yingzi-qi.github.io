Motivation

Something to learn.
Something to contribute.

I’m drawn to work that stretches my thinking and makes careful analysis useful to other people.

What draws me in
Learning / Usefulness / People

Learning through difficult questions.

Personal growth matters to me. I want to keep developing my abilities through unfamiliar subjects and demanding problems, with people I can learn from.

Moving from mathematics and physics into global-health modelling gave me a practical experience of that. I had to build new subject knowledge while putting it to use in research. Independent study and conversations with colleagues helped me contribute while I was still learning.

Making analysis useful.

I’m interested in the point where understanding becomes a decision: what a model tells us, which assumptions matter, and how to explain the result clearly enough for someone else to use it.

That interest connects my research with my independent projects. A research explanation, a comparison tool and an interactive scenario can each give someone a different way into a complicated question.

Keeping people in the picture.

Health is an important thread in my interests. I care about how access, geography and socioeconomic circumstances shape people’s opportunities and outcomes.

Medical technology and evidence-informed healthcare are areas I want to explore further. More broadly, I’m interested in teams where quantitative thinking, thoughtful communication and practical work come together.
